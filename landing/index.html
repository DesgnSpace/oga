Why Oga is different

You keep the agent you already use.

Other tools ask you to move in: a new window, a new chat, a new place to keep your work. Oga adds one server to the agent you already have open, and the delegating starts there.

Nothing to move into

Elsewhere: open their app, start the conversation again, learn where everything lives.

Here: add Oga to your agent, keep the conversation you are in, and hand work out from inside it.

Every account you pay for

Elsewhere: one tool at a time, and its limit is your limit.

Here: work goes to whichever of your accounts has room, so hitting a limit stops one task, not your day.

Sent, not supervised

Elsewhere: panes to watch, each one waiting on you.

Here: a task carries its own brief, the files it may touch, and its own copy of the project. You get told when it lands.

  • T3 Code

    A control app for the agents already on your machine. Oga skips the extra app: you stay in the agent you already have open.

  • Solo

    A workspace your agents share. Oga needs no shared room: each task carries what it needs.

  • Conductor

    Cloud sandboxes that keep coding agents running after you close your laptop. Oga needs no sandbox: work goes out through the agent you already have and comes back to it.

  • Bloom

    One machine, one set of agents. Oga spreads the work across every account you already pay for.

For bigger jobs

Send it away from your files.

Give the work its own branch. It runs in a separate copy of your repository, so nothing changes in your working folder until you merge.

  1. 01

    You ask

    Tell your agent to run it on a branch of its own:

    Take the CSV importer, work on a branch of its own, open a pull request.
  2. 02

    It works apart

    It gets a copy of the repo on its own oga/ branch. Two jobs never fight over the same files.

  3. 03

    You review

    It comes back as a branch you merge, with the pull request link when it opens one.
